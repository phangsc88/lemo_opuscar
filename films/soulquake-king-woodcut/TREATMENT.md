# SoulQuake 0 — 谁才是真正的王？ | Woodcut Print

90 seconds, landscape 1920×1080, 24 fps. The approved 27-clause Mandarin narration and bilingual subtitle timing are retained. The picture is newly directed as a sequence of original relief-print plates: black ink, warm laid paper, and one oxblood colour plate reserved for God's covenant promise and its fulfilment in Christ.

## Three structures considered

1. **Seven independent plates.** Clear wordless-novel rhythm, but the episodes could feel like unrelated illustrations.
2. **A knife travels across one long block.** Strong craft focus, but too close to a continuous journey and too weak for the changes in argument.
3. **Chosen: the same crown block is repeatedly recut.** The crown begins as the answer people want, fractures under Saul and David, then its negative space becomes a covenant seal, cross and open tomb. Each new impression preserves scars from the earlier carving. This gives the sermon one visual object whose meaning changes.

Logline: A crown carved to carry our hopes breaks under human kings, then becomes the negative space through which the true eternal King is revealed.

Arc: desire for a powerful rescuer → demand for a king → failure of human kings → covenant promise → Christ's obedience, death and resurrection → question of trust.

## Benchmark

Study the emotional mass and economy of early wordless woodcut novels and the form-following gouge direction of historical relief prints. Learn black-mass composition, carved light and page rhythm. Copy no existing plate, character, border, pose or title. Every figure, crown, throne, cross, tomb and transition is original and symbolic; no historical likeness is claimed.

## Native move and transitions

The signature transition is the mirrored-block print at 62 seconds: the crown and `王` appear reversed in glossy black wood, paper lays down, a baren pressure circle crosses the sheet, and the peel reveals the true-reading cross/tomb plate. Elsewhere, hard cuts are new impressions with two frames of misregistration. No fades or generic wipes.

Pictures reveal by cuts growing from coarse U-gouges to V-gouge hatching. Light is removed wood: broad white cuts for promise and resurrection, grazing edge cuts on black figures, white carved skin with black features. Hatching follows the form: radial around crown and seal, curved around faces, downward on robes, fall-line on hills. Distant cuts are fine and dense; close cuts are coarse and sparse.

## Colour and text

Near-black ink on warm laid paper. One oxblood red plate appears only at the covenant promise, follows the cross/tomb transformation, and contracts to a small seal at the end. It prints only into carved white areas. Raw wood brown appears briefly during the mirrored-block transition.

Plate titles are carved into the image or letterpressed in the bottom margin. Full scripture remains in narration and bilingual subtitles; in-plate text is limited to references and short words. Chinese uses the retained Noto Serif CJK face with distressed print treatment.

## Sound and cue map

Original D Dorian score at 72 BPM in 3/4, built from low cello/contrabass samples, sparse pizzicato, woodblock and modal struck metal. The earlier chamber cue is not reused as an edit; a new cue follows the plate rhythm. Knife fibres, chips, brayer tack, baren rasp, wet peel and block knocks are procedural. Music and foley duck under narration.

- 0–14: low drone and knife fibres; first knock at the crown reveal.
- 14–31: pizzicato pulse and crowd-like wood taps; thin to near-silence before 1 Samuel 8:7.
- 31–44: two heavy block strikes and a split-wood crack.
- 44–62: oxblood seal impact, modal shift from D minor colour to D Dorian; sustained metal decay.
- 62–74: brayer, paper air, baren rasp and wet peel; the first broad cello interval enters after the true print appears.
- 74–86: near-silence beneath the personal question.
- 86–90: one open fifth and final woodblock knock.

Target −14 LUFS, true peak below −1.5 dBTP. Two near-silences occur before the covenant and before the personal question.
